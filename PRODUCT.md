# Product

## Register

brand

## Platform

web

## Users

The primary audience is Catholic and Catholic-curious young adults in the San Francisco Bay Area, generally arriving on a phone after an Instagram post, shared link, or parish-hall gathering. They need to understand quickly whether the community is for them, what happens at a gathering, and how to attend without social or logistical ambiguity.

The secondary audience is clergy, parish leaders, speakers, ministry partners, supporters, and donors assessing the organization’s seriousness, activity, and credibility.

## Product Purpose

The site turns curiosity into participation. Its main job is to help a prospective attendee find and RSVP for the next verified gathering. When no future event is verified, it must say so clearly and direct visitors to the community channels where an announcement will appear. Secondary goals are joining the ongoing community, understanding the mission, and supporting it financially.

Success means a visitor can understand the offer in ten seconds, find accurate event information without consulting social media, and take one clear next step. Event freshness is a product requirement: expired events can never remain labeled as upcoming.

## Positioning

Catholic Leaders in Action brings young adults together to form their faith, find their people, and put Catholic Social Teaching into concrete action in Bay Area life.

## Conversion & proof

- Primary CTA: **See upcoming events**, becoming **RSVP** when a verified future event is available.
- Secondary CTA: **Join the community** through the existing membership form and WhatsApp community.
- Tertiary CTA: **Support the mission** through the verified Venmo destination, without tax-deductibility claims.
- Ten-second message: **Form your faith. Find your people. Put Catholic social teaching into action.**
- Belief ladder: this is a real local community; gatherings combine friendship, formation, prayer, and dialogue; the program connects faith to service, civic responsibility, and daily vocation; event information is accurate; joining is a clear, low-friction step.
- Proof on hand: six documentary gathering photographs in `assets/images/`; an Archdiocese of San Francisco feature reporting about 50 attendees at the inaugural gathering; a verified public quote from Saul Perez; the official website, Instagram, membership form, WhatsApp community, and Venmo profile listed in the build brief.

## Brand Personality

**Civic, luminous, grounded.** The emotional register is confident, human, contemporary, and reverent without ornament. It should recall cool San Francisco dusk outside a warm parish hall: conviction without stiffness, and friendship without forced cheerfulness.

## Anti-references

The site must not resemble a navy-and-gold church brochure, generic youth-ministry template, luxury editorial magazine, political campaign, startup SaaS landing page, or the familiar cream-serif-terracotta AI aesthetic. Avoid parchment, glassmorphism, gradient text, decorative grid backgrounds, giant rounded cards, ornamental religious motifs, stock cathedral imagery, repeated kickers, and scattered animation.

## Design Principles

1. **Invitation before institution.** Show real people and make the next step socially and logistically clear before explaining the organization in depth.
2. **Useful before beautiful.** Event status, date, location, and RSVP state remain immediately readable and are never hidden behind motion.
3. **Faith becomes action.** Formation, prayer, and action form one narrative sequence, not three interchangeable feature cards.
4. **Documentary credibility.** Real Bay Area gathering photography and precisely sourced facts carry trust; unverified claims and invented testimonials never appear.
5. **One cinematic gesture.** Concentrate motion in the Formation → Prayer → Action story and let the rest of the experience stay calm.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA, with a 7:1 goal for body text. Use semantic landmarks, a visible skip link, strong focus indicators, native controls, 44×44px touch targets, keyboard-operable navigation, descriptive alt text, and layouts that remain usable at 200% zoom. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`; the reduced-motion and no-JavaScript experiences must contain the complete content without empty pinned space or hidden calls to action.
