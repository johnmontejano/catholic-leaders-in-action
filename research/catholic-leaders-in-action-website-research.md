# Catholic Leaders in Action — website research and creative direction

Research date: 2026-08-14 (America/Los_Angeles)

## Executive recommendation

Build a serious, editorial, photo-led landing page that feels like a living invitation into Catholic community, not a generic church bulletin or a luxury-fashion template. The strongest concept is a three-act scroll narrative based on the organization's own public language — **Formation · Action · Prayer** — with the **next valid upcoming event visible immediately** and the longer cinematic story unfolding beneath it.

The site's practical job is conversion and trust: help a prospective attendee understand who the group is, what happens at an event, when and where the next event is, and how to RSVP, join the community, become a member, or donate. Motion should deepen this narrative without delaying those tasks.

The most urgent product issue is freshness. As of this research date, the official landing page still labels the June 2 event “Called to Lead” as “NEXT EVENT,” even though official Archdiocese coverage documents later July 7 and August 5 events. The new site needs a date-aware event model that can never present a past event as upcoming. Sources: [Catholic Leaders in Action official site](https://catholicleadersinaction.org/), [Archdiocese June coverage](https://sfarch.org/catholic-leaders-in-action-young-adults-group-launches-social-teaching-series/), [Archdiocese July follow-up](https://sfarch.org/catholic-lobbyist-calls-for-civic-involvement-at-young-adult-gathering/).

### Unknowns that must not be invented

- The next event after August 5, 2026, including its date, venue, speaker, and RSVP URL.
- Whether CLA is an official Archdiocesan ministry, an affiliated independent group, or another legal/organizational form. The public sources show close Archdiocesan ties but do not settle the legal relationship.
- 501(c)(3) status, EIN, donation deductibility, financial stewardship language, or whether the Venmo account is the preferred long-term donation method.
- Formal age eligibility. The Archdiocese categorizes young-adult ministry as ages 18–40, but CLA's own public landing page says “young Catholics” and “All are welcome”; do not impose an age cutoff without confirmation.
- Approved logo/crest, brand standards, official contact email, accessibility accommodations, privacy terms, photo releases, and permission to reuse the supplied images.
- A complete leadership biography, board/advisory structure, recurring event schedule, and series roadmap.
- Current Instagram posts. Agent Reach's required Instagram route was tested, but the local OpenCLI browser extension was not connected and returned no public profile data; no Instagram facts are used in this note.

## Likely audiences and the questions the page must answer

These are product inferences from the documented program, not organization-supplied personas.

1. **First-time attendee:** Is this for me? What actually happens? Will I know anyone? When/where is the next gathering? Is it free? What should I wear or bring? Is the venue accessible?
2. **Returning attendee/member:** What is the next theme? Who is speaking? What is the related service/action? How do I RSVP, join WhatsApp, or share the event?
3. **Potential donor or sponsor:** What mission and activity am I supporting? Is this the correct payment destination? What impact has the group demonstrated?
4. **Parish, ministry, clergy, or speaker partner:** Who leads the group? How does an event combine formation and action? How can we collaborate or host?

The strongest trust proof currently available is concrete and human: two documented gatherings of about 50 attendees; named founders, speakers, panelists, and chaplain; recognizable parish venues; and a recurring format that combines social connection, serious formation, prayer, and service.

## Recommended information architecture

Keep it as a focused landing page with optional event-detail/archive routes:

1. **Hero / next event:** one sentence of mission, a single dominant community image, next-event date/time/venue, and primary `RSVP` button. Secondary action: `Join the community`.
2. **Short trust strip:** `Archdiocese of San Francisco` relationship phrased exactly as approved; `7-part Catholic Social Teaching series`; `~50 at each of the first two documented gatherings` (only while appropriately contextualized and sourced).
3. **Formation · Action · Prayer:** three cinematic chapters explaining what attendees experience. Keep the copy specific: keynote/lecture, panel/Q&A, social hour, prayer, and a linked ministry action.
4. **Upcoming events:** one featured next event plus a clean chronological list. If none is confirmed, show “The next gathering is being finalized” with a notification/join CTA — never a past event.
5. **Past events / the series journey:** a seven-theme progress rail or archive with dates, speakers, recaps, and related action. Never imply all seven are complete.
6. **Community photo story:** editorial mosaic using the supplied images, captions when identities are confirmed, and a restrained horizontal rail on large screens.
7. **Leadership / ecclesial trust:** founders and chaplain only after names, titles, headshots, and relationship language are approved.
8. **Ways to participate:** RSVP, become a member, WhatsApp, volunteer/action, invite a friend, donate.
9. **Footer:** contact, Instagram, Venmo, privacy/photo policy, venue/accessibility contact, and Archdiocesan relationship disclaimer if needed.

## Event content model and freshness rules

The event system is the core functional requirement, even if the first version stores content in a local data file rather than a CMS.

Recommended fields:

- `title`, `theme`, `seriesNumber`, `status` (`draft`, `upcoming`, `soldOut`, `cancelled`, `past`)
- `startDateTime`, `endDateTime`, and `timezone` (America/Los_Angeles)
- `venueName`, street address, map/directions URL, transit/parking note
- short promise (“what you'll leave with”), detailed agenda, dress/cost/age/audience guidance
- keynote speaker and bio; moderator; panelists
- related ministry/action partner and a concrete follow-through CTA
- RSVP URL, capacity/waitlist state, calendar download, share URL
- accessibility/contact note and language availability
- hero image, focal point, alt text, caption/credit, and permission status
- recap URL, gallery, and outcome notes once complete

Rules:

- Determine “next” from a future timestamp in the configured timezone; never hand-label an event as next.
- Separate upcoming and past events automatically. Move expired events into the archive without deleting them.
- Do not hide critical date/time/location/RSVP content behind animation, hover, or an image carousel.
- Provide a graceful empty state when no future event is confirmed.
- Add valid `Event` structured data for each public event, but only from confirmed content.
- Give nontechnical organizers a low-friction update path and assign an owner plus a pre-event publishing checklist.

## Visual direction: “Sacred civic editorial”

The visual target should feel mature, hospitable, local, and consequential — a contemporary Catholic civic movement rather than a corporate conference or ornate church brochure.

### Design language

- **Palette:** parchment/ivory background, near-black or midnight navy text, oxblood/burgundy as the primary institutional accent, muted antique gold used sparingly, and a soft sage accent drawn from the event photography. Avoid a black-and-gold luxury cliché.
- **Typography:** a dignified editorial serif for display headlines paired with a highly legible humanist sans for navigation, event metadata, and body copy. The serif should feel literary and civic, not bridal or fashion-editorial.
- **Layout:** strong 12-column editorial grid, generous white space, asymmetric photo crops, hairline rules, small uppercase metadata, and large but controlled headlines. Use real paper/grain texture at very low opacity; no faux parchment scrolls, clip-art crosses, glowing stained glass, or generic AI religious imagery.
- **Catholic symbolism:** subtle and structural — a cross-shaped grid intersection, a restrained four-point star, or liturgical cadence — not decorative overload. Use an approved mark only.
- **Tone:** serious without being severe; youthful without being trendy; faith-forward without becoming visually sentimental.

## Motion direction: cinematic, scenic, and purposeful

Use one continuous narrative rather than unrelated effects:

1. **Opening:** the community image resolves from a narrow cinematic crop into a wider scene while the mission line reveals in two measured beats. The event CTA is already present and usable.
2. **Three acts:** `Formation`, `Action`, and `Prayer` move through a pinned editorial sequence on large screens. Each word anchors one real photograph and one concrete sentence. On mobile this becomes a normal vertical sequence.
3. **Series journey:** a thin progress line traces the seven Catholic Social Teaching themes; completed events gain dates and recaps, the next event is emphasized, and unconfirmed themes remain explicitly “to be announced.”
4. **Community rail:** paired portrait/landscape photographs slide horizontally on desktop, driven by vertical scroll, with native swipe/scroll-snap on mobile. Do not trap the scroll.
5. **Closing invitation:** the group photo settles into view with a short call to participate and the RSVP/join/donate actions.

Prefer transform/opacity/mask reveals, subtle 1–3% image drift, and restrained stagger. Avoid custom cursors, autoplay audio, long loaders, perpetual marquee text, heavy WebGL, excessive parallax, and scroll-hijacking. The content is the spectacle.

GSAP's official ScrollTrigger documentation supports viewport triggers, timelines, scrubbing, pinning, responsive setups, and native-scroll compatibility; it also recommends responsive `matchMedia()` setups. `gsap.matchMedia()` can branch mobile/desktop behavior and honor `prefers-reduced-motion`, reverting animation setups automatically when conditions change. Sources: [GSAP ScrollTrigger docs](https://gsap.com/docs/v3/Plugins/ScrollTrigger/), [GSAP matchMedia docs](https://gsap.com/docs/v3/GSAP/gsap.matchMedia()/).

Image treatment:

- Normalize white balance and exposure across the set; use a restrained warm grade and preserve natural skin tones.
- Generate responsive AVIF/WebP derivatives while retaining originals. Never upscale small files beyond what their detail supports.
- Store focal point, caption, credit, and permission/release status with every image.
- Use art-directed `<picture>` crops for desktop/mobile when needed and include intrinsic dimensions or `aspect-ratio` to prevent layout shift.
- Do not animate the hero from blur to sharp if that delays perceived load. Above-the-fold imagery should be immediately discoverable and should not be lazy-loaded if it becomes the LCP element.
- Verify names before identifying people in captions. Generic alt text should describe the scene and purpose, not speculate about identities.

Google's current performance guidance defines a good LCP as 2.5 seconds or less for at least 75% of visits, recommends making the LCP resource discoverable early, advises against lazy-loading the LCP image, and allows carefully applied high fetch priority for the likely LCP image. Its CLS guidance says images/video should include width and height or reserve space with `aspect-ratio`; transform-based animation avoids re-layout. Sources: [Optimize LCP](https://web.dev/articles/optimize-lcp), [Optimize CLS](https://web.dev/articles/optimize-cls).

## Accessibility and performance guardrails

- All core content and actions must exist in semantic HTML before animation runs. If JavaScript or GSAP fails, the page remains complete and usable.
- Honor `prefers-reduced-motion` with `gsap.matchMedia()`: remove parallax, pinning, scrubbed timelines, large zooms, and horizontal movement; use immediate state changes or short opacity transitions instead.
- Consider a visible site-level “Reduce motion” control for the cinematic version, not only OS preference.
- W3C's WCAG 2.2 guidance says nonessential motion animation triggered by interaction must be disableable unless essential, specifically noting that scroll-triggered motion and parallax can cause dizziness, nausea, and headaches. [W3C Understanding SC 2.3.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html)
- Preserve native scrolling; no scroll-jacking. Keep skip links, visible focus, logical heading order, keyboard-operable navigation, meaningful alt text, and adequate contrast.
- Keep event metadata text selectable and readable; do not render it solely inside canvas, SVG paths without accessible text, or image assets.
- Use responsive images and conservative media budgets. The five smaller supplied images should remain contained cards/mosaic tiles.
- Lazy-load only below-the-fold media. Reserve layout space for every image and dynamic event module.
- Mobile gets normal vertical flow, native swipe/scroll-snap for any gallery, and fewer simultaneous animations.
- Test 320px through wide desktop, keyboard-only, screen reader landmarks, reduced motion, 200% zoom, low-end mobile throttling, and event links/empty states.

## Content and conversion guidance

### Recommended message hierarchy

1. **Identity:** a community of young Catholics rooted in Christ.
2. **Promise:** form leaders, build community, and bring faith into action.
3. **Experience:** social connection + serious Catholic formation + prayer + a concrete act of service/advocacy.
4. **Immediate invitation:** next event with all logistics and RSVP.
5. **Ongoing belonging:** membership and WhatsApp.
6. **Support:** donation and partnership, with accurate legal language only after verification.

### Copy principles

- Use the organization's confirmed phrases where helpful, but do not repeat the entire page as slogans.
- Prefer concrete verbs: gather, learn, pray, serve, advocate, accompany, lead.
- Avoid inflated claims such as “transforming the Bay Area,” “official ministry,” “movement of thousands,” or “tax-deductible” until documented.
- Explain Catholic Social Teaching briefly for newcomers; do not assume the phrase is self-explanatory.
- Make the event feel socially safe: publish a short “What to expect” agenda and practical arrival details.

### Primary calls to action

- `RSVP for the next gathering` — dominant whenever a valid future event exists.
- `Join the community` — membership/WhatsApp choice presented clearly.
- `See the series` — secondary journey/archive action.
- `Support the mission` — lower on the page, linked to the verified public payment destination only after owner confirmation.

## Owner questions before production copy is finalized

1. What is the next confirmed event after August 5, and where is the canonical source of event data?
2. What exact wording may the site use for its relationship to the Archdiocese of San Francisco?
3. Who owns website updates, and how many days before each event must details be published?
4. Is CLA legally a nonprofit/tax-exempt organization, and what donation/disclosure language is approved?
5. Are the supplied photos cleared for public web, social sharing, and cropping? Who receives credit?
6. What are the approved logo, colors, fonts, contact email, privacy statement, and safeguarding/photo policies?
7. Are events free, age-limited, registration-required, wheelchair accessible, bilingual, or capacity-limited?
8. Which organizer biographies and headshots are approved?
9. Should past talks include recordings, summaries, reflection prompts, or links to ministry actions?
10. What success metrics matter: RSVPs, attendance, membership sign-ups, WhatsApp joins, volunteers, or donations?
